Tire Size Explained: Width, Aspect Ratio & Diameter

A tire code looks cryptic until you break it into three measurements. Once you do, most tire-size math becomes straightforward.

How to read a tire size

In 225/45R17, 225 is the nominal section width in millimeters. The 45 is the aspect ratio, meaning the sidewall height is 45% of the section width. The 17 is the wheel diameter in inches.

Calculating overall diameter

First calculate sidewall height in millimeters, divide by 25.4 to convert it to inches, double it for the top and bottom sidewalls, then add the wheel diameter.

diameter = wheel + 2 × (width × aspect ratio ÷ 100 ÷ 25.4)

Why changing diameter affects the speedometer

The speedometer expects a certain distance traveled per wheel revolution. A taller tire covers more ground per revolution, so actual speed is higher than indicated. A shorter tire covers less ground, so actual speed is lower.

Fitment is more than diameter

Section width, wheel width, offset, suspension shape, alignment and body clearance all matter. Two tires with nearly identical diameter can fit very differently if their widths are different.

A practical comparison workflow

Start with the exact current size printed on the tire, then enter the proposed size in the calculator. Compare diameter and speedometer change first. For example, 225/45R17 works out to roughly 24.97 inches in diameter; 245/40R18 is roughly 25.72 inches. At an indicated 60 mph, the taller size would imply around 61.8 mph if the original calibration was correct. This is a geometric estimate rather than a measured rolling circumference.

Next, check the tire maker's recommended wheel-width range and published overall width. Verify the vehicle's load and speed requirements. Finally, measure available space at the front and rear of the tire while steering and through suspension travel. A diameter comparison cannot prove fitment, especially when the new tire is wider. Do not assume a nominal tire code makes two brands identical in physical dimensions.
