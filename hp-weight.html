Reading the ratio

lb/hp = vehicle weight ÷ horsepower
hp/ton = horsepower ÷ (weight ÷ 2,000)

Lower pounds per horsepower means each horsepower has less mass to accelerate. Horsepower per ton expresses the same relationship in the opposite direction.

Worked example

A 3,000-pound car with 300 horsepower and a 4,000-pound car with 400 horsepower both equal 10 lb/hp. That does not mean their launches, lap times or top-end acceleration will match.

Compare like with like

Use the same horsepower basis and the same weight convention. Mixing advertised crank horsepower with wheel horsepower or curb weight with race weight distorts the comparison.

What the ratio cannot predict

Traction, gearing, torque curve, shift strategy, transmission behavior and aerodynamic drag are outside the ratio. Use power-to-weight as a screening metric rather than a complete performance model.

Method & verification

The ratio is direct arithmetic. Input consistency is the important part: note whether the horsepower is crank or wheel and exactly what the entered weight includes.
