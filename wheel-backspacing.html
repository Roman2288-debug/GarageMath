Offset and backspacing are two views of the same geometry

Offset is measured from the wheel centerline in millimeters. Backspacing is measured from the hub mounting face to the inner wheel edge in inches. The nominal conversion uses wheel width divided by two, plus offset converted to inches.

The second result adds one inch to nominal wheel width before dividing by two. That approximates the extra outer-lip width commonly present beyond the bead seats, but real wheel construction varies.

When brake or suspension clearance is tight, measure the actual wheel. Published backspacing from the wheel manufacturer is better than a generic lip estimate.

Worked example: why two backspacing figures appear

An 8-inch nominal wheel at ET25 has a centerline 4 inches from its bead-seat edge. Convert 25 mm to about 0.98 inch and add it: nominal backspacing is about 4.98 inches. If the physical rim is approximately one inch wider than its nominal bead-seat width, an outer-lip estimate produces about 5.48 inches. Neither figure overrides a manufacturer's measured specification.

Use the right measurement for the job

Backspacing is taken from the hub-mounting face to the innermost physical edge of the wheel. Nominal wheel width describes the space between tire bead seats, not necessarily the full outside-to-outside width. That distinction matters when a suspension arm or brake part is close to the wheel. For a wheel already in hand, lay a straightedge across the inner lip and measure down to the mounting pad. Also inspect spoke-to-caliper clearance, which backspacing alone cannot predict.

Why might measured backspacing differ?

This is a nominal conversion from wheel width and offset. Actual lip dimensions and the measuring method can differ; verify the wheel maker’s specification.
