Injector sizing formula

injector lb/hr = horsepower × BSFC ÷ (injector count × duty cycle)

BSFC, or brake-specific fuel consumption, estimates how much fuel mass is needed per horsepower-hour. Naturally aspirated gasoline engines often use a lower BSFC assumption than boosted combinations, but actual requirements depend on the engine, fuel, tune and target air-fuel ratio.

About the cc/min conversion

The cc/min result uses an approximate gasoline conversion of 10.5 cc/min per lb/hr. Injector manufacturers may rate flow at different pressures and fuels, so always use the manufacturer’s data for final sizing.

Fuel-system sizing and tuning affect engine safety. Treat this as a planning estimate and verify injector data, base pressure, fuel type and tuning requirements.

Worked example: duty cycle changes the target

A 400-horsepower gasoline engine with eight injectors and an assumed BSFC of 0.50 lb per horsepower-hour needs 200 lb of fuel per hour in total. At an 80% maximum duty cycle, each injector needs at least 31.25 lb/hr by this simplified calculation. At 60% duty cycle, the result rises to about 41.7 lb/hr. The difference is a planning margin, not proof that a particular injector will deliver that flow in the vehicle.

Verify the fuel system as a whole

Injector flow ratings depend on pressure differential and fuel properties. Ethanol blends can require more fuel volume than gasoline for comparable power, and boosted applications must maintain the intended pressure across the injector. Pump capacity, wiring, regulator behavior and injector characterization in the tune also matter. Do not buy an injector solely from a rounded cc/min conversion; check the manufacturer's flow data at your operating pressure and review the choice with a qualified tuner.

Is the calculated flow rate enough to order injectors?

Treat it as a planning minimum under the entered BSFC and duty cycle. Check fuel pressure, injector rating conditions, fuel type and tune with a qualified tuner.
