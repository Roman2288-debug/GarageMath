Injector sizing formula

injector lb/hr = horsepower × BSFC ÷ (injector count × duty cycle)

BSFC estimates fuel mass needed per horsepower-hour. Duty cycle reserves headroom by limiting how much of each engine cycle the injector is expected to stay open.

Worked example

A 400 hp engine with eight injectors, 0.50 BSFC and 80% duty cycle needs about 31.25 lb/hr per injector under this simplified model. Lowering the allowed duty cycle increases the required injector size.

Why cc/min is approximate

GarageMath uses a rough gasoline conversion for convenience. Actual injector flow depends on fuel properties and pressure differential. Manufacturer flow data at the intended operating pressure is the number to use for final selection.

Verify the entire fuel system

Fuel pump capacity, voltage, regulator behavior, base pressure, boost reference, wiring and injector characterization in the tune all matter. Ethanol blends can require materially different fuel volume from gasoline for the same power target.

Fuel-system sizing and tuning affect engine safety. Treat this as a planning estimate and verify the combination with manufacturer data and a qualified tuner.

Which horsepower belongs in the input?

Use engine (crank) horsepower when BSFC describes fuel consumption per engine horsepower-hour. A wheel-horsepower target paired with an engine-based BSFC understates the modeled fuel demand. Do not insert a universal drivetrain-loss percentage: use a documented engine-power estimate and keep that assumption with the result.

Run a range before choosing a part

At 400 engine hp, eight injectors and 80% duty, a hypothetical BSFC of 0.50 gives 31.25 lb/hr each. A BSFC of 0.60 gives 37.50 lb/hr each. At 70% duty and 0.60 BSFC the estimate rises to 42.86 lb/hr. These are example assumptions, not recommended BSFC settings for a particular engine or fuel.

Compare those results with the manufacturer’s flow rating at the actual operating pressure differential. Record fuel, rating pressure and tuning data alongside the part number. A larger nominal injector is not automatically a better-running injector, and the approximate gasoline cc/min output does not convert this into an ethanol or direct-injection sizing tool.
