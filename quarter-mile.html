The equations are empirical shortcuts

ET ≈ 5.825 × ∛(weight ÷ horsepower)
trap mph ≈ 234 × ∛(horsepower ÷ weight)

These equations compress a complicated vehicle and track into two inputs. That makes them useful for rough comparisons and sanity checks, but not precise predictions.

Worked example

A 3,500-pound race-weight car with 350 horsepower lands around the low-12-second range and roughly the high-100-mph range under this shortcut. Real results can move with traction, shift quality, gearing, weather and aerodynamic drag.

Use race weight consistently

Include the driver, fuel and equipment carried during the pass. Keep horsepower basis consistent too: do not compare advertised crank horsepower for one car with wheel horsepower for another and expect the estimate to remain meaningful.

ET and trap speed react differently

Elapsed time is especially sensitive to launch and traction. Trap speed is often less sensitive to the first 60 feet, but it can still change with weather, elevation, aerodynamics, gearing and power delivery.

Performance testing belongs on a controlled course or drag strip. This estimator is not a public-road speed or safety tool.

Use changes as comparisons, not promises

At 3,500 lb and 350 hp, the displayed equations give about 12.55 seconds and 108.6 mph. Increasing the entered power to 400 hp at the same weight gives about 12.00 seconds and 113.0 mph. That is a model comparison; the tool has not predicted a launch, shifts or a particular track.

What to record with a real time slip

Record race weight, the horsepower measurement basis, the 60-foot time, elapsed time and trap speed. If two passes have similar trap speed but different elapsed times, inspect the launch and intermediate times before concluding that engine output changed. Conditions and driving still complicate that comparison.

This page deliberately avoids converting a single pass into a claimed dyno number. The fixed coefficients are approximate conventions, and the estimator has no calibration for your specific vehicle. Use the same inputs and measurement basis when comparing proposed changes.
