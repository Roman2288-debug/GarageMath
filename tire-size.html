How the tire-size math works

A metric size such as 225/45R17 means a nominal 225 mm section width, a sidewall height equal to 45% of that width and a 17-inch wheel. Overall diameter is the wheel plus two sidewalls.

diameter = wheel + 2 × (width × aspect ratio ÷ 100 ÷ 25.4)

What the result means

A taller tire covers more distance per revolution. If the vehicle still assumes the original tire size, actual road speed rises relative to indicated speed. Ride height changes by approximately half the difference in diameter. Effective gearing changes at the same time.

Worked example

225/45R17 is about 24.97 inches in nominal diameter. 245/40R18 is about 25.72 inches. That is roughly a 3% increase, about 0.37 inch of ride-height increase and about 61.8 mph actual speed at 60 mph indicated if the original calibration was accurate.

What this cannot prove

Printed tire size is not an exact physical measurement. Tire model, wheel width, load and inflation change measured dimensions. Width, offset, suspension, body clearance, load capacity and wheel-speed system requirements still need to be checked.

Before you buy

  • Check the vehicle placard or manufacturer-approved sizes.
  • Compare the exact tire model’s published overall diameter and section width.
  • Verify load and speed ratings.
  • Measure physical clearance at steering lock and through suspension travel.
  • For AWD vehicles, follow the manufacturer’s allowable circumference guidance.

Use the comparison to check your assumptions

The default sizes differ in diameter by about 2.98%, but that percentage is not an approval rule. A nominally similar diameter can still come with the wrong load rating, wheel-width requirement or insufficient clearance. There is no universal percentage on this page that declares a replacement safe for every vehicle.

What happens to distance readings?

If distance is derived from the same unchanged wheel-revolution calibration, a taller replacement tire also increases actual distance per indicated mile. With the default sizes, 100 indicated miles correspond to roughly 102.98 geometric miles. An MPG calculation based on the uncorrected distance would inherit that error. This assumes the vehicle’s calibration originally matched the old tires; GPS or manufacturer calibration checks can help establish the actual relationship.

Method & verification

The calculation uses the exact international conversion of 1 inch = 25.4 mm. Final dimensions and replacement suitability should be verified against the exact tire and vehicle specifications.
