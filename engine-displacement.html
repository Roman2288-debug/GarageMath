Displacement formula

displacement = π ÷ 4 × bore² × stroke × cylinders

With bore and stroke entered in inches, the result is cubic inches. GarageMath converts the same swept volume to cubic centimeters and liters.

Worked example

A 3.50-inch bore, 3.50-inch stroke and four cylinders produce about 134.7 cubic inches, roughly 2,208 cc or 2.21 liters.

Use the finished measurements

An overbore changes displacement, so use the finished cylinder bore after machining when accuracy matters. Stroke is crankshaft stroke, not connecting-rod length. Displacement is swept volume only; chamber, piston and gasket volumes belong in compression-ratio calculations.

What displacement does not tell you

Displacement alone does not predict horsepower, torque, airflow or compression ratio. It is a foundational engine dimension, not a performance rating.

How much does an overbore actually add?

Keep stroke and cylinder count fixed and displacement changes with the square of bore. An eight-cylinder engine with a 4.000-inch bore and 3.480-inch stroke is 349.85 cubic inches. Increasing bore to 4.030 inches produces 355.12 cubic inches: 5.27 cubic inches more, or about 1.51%. A “.030 over” bore therefore does not mean a 3% increase in displacement.

Build a measurement sheet before calculating

Record finished bore, crankshaft stroke and cylinder count separately. Convert millimeters to inches by dividing by 25.4; do not enter 86 mm as 86 inches. For example, 86 mm bore and 86 mm stroke are about 3.386 inches each. Four cylinders at those dimensions sweep about 1,998 cc.

Run the calculation for both the original and proposed dimensions, then compare the difference. This helps describe an engine configuration; it does not establish whether a block can accept the proposed machining. Wall thickness and the machinist’s inspection are separate questions.
