Displacement formula

displacement = π ÷ 4 × bore² × stroke × cylinders

Bore and stroke must use the same units. With both in inches, the result is cubic inches. GarageMath converts that result to cubic centimeters and liters.

What displacement tells you

Displacement describes the volume swept by all pistons as they travel through their strokes. It does not directly tell you horsepower, compression ratio or airflow demand, but it is a foundational number for all three.

Worked example: a four-cylinder engine

For a 3.50-inch bore, 3.50-inch stroke and four cylinders, the swept volume is π ÷ 4 × 3.50² × 3.50 × 4, or about 134.7 cubic inches. Using 16.387 cubic centimeters per cubic inch, that is about 2,208 cc or 2.21 liters. The result represents all cylinders together, not the volume of one cylinder.

Measure the finished engine

Use the actual finished bore after machining, especially with an overbore. An extra 0.030 inch on each cylinder changes the total even though the advertised engine family name stays the same. Stroke must be the crankshaft stroke, not connecting-rod length. Displacement is swept volume only; combustion-chamber volume and piston shape belong in compression-ratio calculations. Use measured dimensions when ordering pistons or comparing a rebuilt engine with its factory specification.

Does displacement tell me how much power an engine makes?

No. This is swept volume from bore, stroke and cylinder count. Airflow, compression, fueling and operating speed also affect output.
