GEARING GUIDE

Gear Ratio, RPM & Tire Diameter Explained

Gearing is a chain. Engine speed passes through the selected transmission gear, then the final drive, then the tire converts wheel rotation into road speed.

Transmission ratio and final drive

A transmission gear ratio multiplies engine torque before sending it to the differential. The final-drive ratio multiplies it again. Multiplying the two gives the overall ratio between engine and axle for that gear.

How tire diameter changes effective gearing

A taller tire travels farther per revolution, effectively making the gearing taller. That lowers engine RPM at a given road speed but also reduces mechanical leverage at the tire. A shorter tire does the opposite.

The common road-speed formula

MPH ≈ RPM × tire diameter ÷ (gear ratio × final drive × 336)

The formula is theoretical. Converter slip, clutch slip and loaded tire diameter can shift the real number.

Choosing a final-drive ratio

Numerically higher axle ratios generally increase engine RPM at cruise and increase torque multiplication. Numerically lower ratios generally reduce cruise RPM. The best choice depends on transmission ratios, tire size, powerband and how the vehicle is used.

Work backward from your real cruising speed

Suppose a vehicle has 26-inch tires, a 0.75:1 overdrive and a 3.73:1 axle. The formula predicts about 2,530 rpm at 70 mph. A 4.10:1 axle with the same gear and tire predicts about 2,780 rpm. This gives a concrete comparison before buying gears, but it does not predict fuel economy or acceleration by itself.

Check your transmission's actual ratio chart: “sixth gear” does not identify a numerical ratio. If an automatic converter does not lock, observed engine speed may be higher than the theoretical figure. A tire's loaded radius also differs from its unloaded nominal diameter. Record GPS speed and steady tachometer RPM on a safe drive, then compare the observed result with the calculation. Use that difference when evaluating other gearing options.
