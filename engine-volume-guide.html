Build an engine volume sheet before calculating compression

Keep swept volume separate from chamber, piston, gasket and deck volume, then use a complete measurement sheet to compare engine configurations.

By GarageMathUpdated October 9, 2026Illustrative measurements, no vehicle-specific fitment claims

Start with bore and stroke

Bore is cylinder diameter; stroke is piston travel. Connecting-rod length is a separate measurement. Use the finished dimensions rather than an unverified engine-family nickname, particularly after machining.

The Displacement Calculator accepts inches or millimeters for bore and stroke. Keep both dimensions in the chosen unit. An 86 mm bore and 86 mm stroke over four cylinders produce about 1,998 cc. Entering 86 as inches would describe an entirely different object.

Swept volume per cylinder = π ÷ 4 × bore² × stroke
Total swept volume = per-cylinder volume × cylinder count

Swept volume and clearance volume answer different questions

Displacement is the volume swept by piston travel. Static compression ratio compares the volume at the bottom of the stroke with the volume at the top. It therefore needs both swept volume and the remaining clearance volume.

Do not add chamber volume to total engine displacement. Do not multiply all clearance volumes by cylinder count before entering a per-cylinder compression calculator. Each chamber, piston, gasket and deck input refers to one cylinder.

A complete per-cylinder example

These illustrative values describe a measurement exercise, not a parts recommendation.

Example volume sheet
InputValueMeaning
Bore / stroke4.000 / 3.480 in716.62 cc swept per cylinder

Piston dish+5 ccAdds clearance volume

Total clearance79.93 ccSum of the four clearance contributions

Static compression is (716.62 + 79.93) ÷ 79.93, approximately 9.97:1. The sum is evaluated using the unrounded volumes; displayed values are rounded for readability.

Use the correct signs and measurement state

GarageMath treats a dish or valve-relief volume as positive and a dome as negative. Check a piston supplier’s convention before transcribing the number. Enter zero only when the net piston volume really is zero.

Use compressed gasket thickness and the actual gasket bore. The deck field models a piston at or below the deck, with zero at deck height and a positive clearance below it. A piston above the deck needs a measurement model beyond the current field’s scope. Avoid disguising that configuration as a positive below-deck measurement.

Change one input at a time

Keeping the example dimensions fixed and increasing chamber volume from 64 to 68 cc lowers the calculated ratio from about 9.97:1 to 9.54:1. That isolates the chamber change. If you also change the piston or gasket, record a separate combination so you can explain why the result moved.

An overbore affects swept volume too. At a fixed 3.480-inch stroke and eight cylinders, moving from 4.000 to 4.030 inches adds roughly 5.27 cubic inches of total displacement. It does not establish whether that bore is suitable for the block; machining suitability requires its own inspection.

What the sheet establishes

The volume sheet makes the geometric result reproducible. It does not determine a fuel-octane requirement, dynamic compression, cranking pressure or a tuning limit. Cam timing, chamber design, operating conditions and other variables remain outside static geometry.

Keep the source and measurement date beside each input. If a chamber or piston volume is an assumption, mark it. A plausible ratio with undocumented inputs is weaker than an auditable calculation with an honest uncertainty.

Calculation basis

These examples use cylinder-volume geometry and the international inch definition. See NIST’s length-unit reference and GarageMath methodology. Final component dimensions should come from measurements and the exact manufacturers’ specifications.
