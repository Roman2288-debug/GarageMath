What wheel offset changes

Offset is measured from the wheel centerline to the hub-mounting face. Positive offset generally pulls the wheel inward, but width changes the position of both edges at the same time.

inner position = wheel width ÷ 2 + offset
outer position = wheel width ÷ 2 − offset

Worked example: 8-inch ET40 to 9-inch ET35

One extra inch of width adds 12.7 mm to each side before offset is considered. Dropping offset by 5 mm shifts the wheel outward. The combination moves the inner edge about 7.7 mm closer to suspension and the outer edge about 17.7 mm farther toward the fender.

What this cannot prove

The result describes wheel-edge geometry. It does not know tire section width, tire shoulder shape, spoke-to-caliper clearance, hub bore, bolt pattern, alignment, steering angle, suspension travel or body shape.

Before you buy

  • Confirm bolt pattern, hub bore, load rating and lug-seat type.
  • Check the exact tire’s section width and approved wheel-width range.
  • Measure current fender and suspension clearance.
  • Check steering lock and suspension compression.
  • Use a brake template or test fit when caliper clearance is close.

Method & verification

The position change is simple geometry once width and offset are known. Final fitment must be verified on the specific vehicle because tire, brake, suspension and body dimensions are outside the equation.
