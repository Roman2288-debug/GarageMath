Gearing formula

MPH ≈ RPM × tire diameter ÷ (gear ratio × final drive × 336)

The reverse form solves for RPM. The constant 336 combines the inch, mile and minute conversions used in this common automotive shortcut.

Worked example

With a 26-inch tire, 0.75:1 top gear and 3.73 final drive, theoretical engine speed at 70 mph is about 2,530 RPM. Switching only the axle ratio to 4.10 raises the estimate to roughly 2,780 RPM.

Why measured RPM can differ

Torque-converter slip, clutch slip, tire deflection, tire growth and tachometer error can move the real result away from the theoretical number. Loaded tire diameter or revolutions-per-mile data may better represent road use than nominal sidewall geometry.

Before choosing a gear ratio

  • Use the ratio of the actual transmission gear selected.
  • Confirm final-drive ratio from reliable specifications.
  • Use realistic tire diameter after any tire-size change.
  • Consider converter lockup or clutch slip when comparing to real RPM.
  • Compare several cruise speeds rather than one point.
