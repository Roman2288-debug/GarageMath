Gearing formula

MPH ≈ RPM × tire diameter ÷ (gear ratio × final drive × 336)

The constant 336 converts inches and minutes into miles per hour. The reverse calculation estimates engine RPM at a selected road speed.

Why real RPM can differ

Automatic transmission converter slip, clutch slip, tire growth and actual loaded tire diameter can move real-world results away from the theoretical number.

Worked example: cruise RPM

With a 26-inch tire, 0.75:1 top gear and 3.73:1 final drive, theoretical engine speed at 70 mph is about 2,530 rpm. Changing only the final drive to 4.10:1 raises that to roughly 2,780 rpm. The shorter axle gear increases engine speed at the same road speed; it does not tell you by itself how much fuel the vehicle will use.

Use the correct gear and tire measurement

Enter the transmission ratio for the gear actually selected, not the number of gears in the transmission. A 1.00 direct gear and a 0.75 overdrive give very different answers. Tire sidewall sizes provide a starting diameter, but loaded rolling diameter and manufacturer revolutions-per-mile data may better represent road use. An unlocked torque converter may add slip, so compare the estimate with a steady GPS speed and tachometer reading before using it to choose axle gearing.

Why does actual RPM differ from this estimate?

Automatic transmission converter slip, tire growth or loaded rolling radius, and inaccurate tachometer readings can change observed RPM. Use the correct gear and final-drive ratios.
