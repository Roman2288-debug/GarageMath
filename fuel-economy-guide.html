FUEL COST GUIDE

Fuel cost & MPG math

MPG becomes useful for budgeting when you turn it into gallons used and dollars spent.

Trip costCost per mileAnnual comparison

The two basic equations

gallons used = miles driven ÷ MPG
fuel cost = gallons used × price per gallon

If you drive 300 miles at 25 MPG, the trip uses about 12 gallons. At $3.50 per gallon, fuel cost is about $42.

Cost per mile

fuel cost per mile = price per gallon ÷ MPG

At $3.50 per gallon and 25 MPG, fuel cost is about $0.14 per mile. This makes route and vehicle comparisons easier because you can multiply the per-mile number by any distance.

Annual fuel cost

Use actual yearly mileage when possible. A 12,000-mile year at 25 MPG uses about 480 gallons. At $3.50 per gallon, that is roughly $1,680 in fuel.

Why MPG gains are not linear

An increase from 10 to 15 MPG saves far more fuel than an increase from 30 to 35 MPG over the same distance because gallons per mile is the inverse of MPG. Comparing annual gallons or cost per mile avoids that confusion.

Choosing realistic MPG

The calculation is only as good as the MPG entered. Highway ratings can overstate a stop-and-go commute. Towing, roof loads, aggressive acceleration, cold starts, idling, high speed and tire pressure can change consumption substantially. If you have several fill-ups of real data, total miles divided by total gallons is usually a stronger planning input than one unusually good trip-computer reading.

Towing and special trips

For a towing trip, use the MPG you actually expect while towing rather than your unloaded average. If you do not know it, run a range of scenarios. A 300-mile trip at 25 MPG uses 12 gallons; at 20 MPG it uses 15 gallons. At $3.50 per gallon, that difference is $10.50.

What fuel cost does not include

Fuel cost is not total vehicle operating cost. Maintenance, tires, depreciation, insurance, tolls, parking and financing can be larger than the fuel difference between two vehicles. GarageMath keeps those categories separate so the fuel calculation stays transparent.

Common mistakes

  • Using one-way mileage for a round trip.
  • Using highway MPG for mostly city driving.
  • Assuming fuel price stays fixed for a year.
  • Comparing percentage MPG improvement instead of gallons saved.
  • Treating fuel cost as total ownership cost.
