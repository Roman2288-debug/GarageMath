Fuel-cost formula

gallons = miles ÷ MPG
cost = gallons × price per gallon
cost per mile = price per gallon ÷ MPG

Worked example

A 300-mile trip at 25 MPG uses 12 gallons. At $3.50 per gallon, the fuel estimate is $42, or about $0.14 per mile. At 20 MPG, the same trip uses 15 gallons and costs $52.50.

Choose realistic inputs

Use a fuel-economy figure that matches the trip. Highway MPG can overstate stop-and-go driving, and towing, idling, weather, speed and tire pressure can change consumption. Several fill-ups of real-world mileage and gallons are usually more useful for budgeting than one unusually good trip-computer reading.

What this does not include

This is fuel only. Tolls, parking, maintenance, tires, insurance, depreciation and financing are separate costs.

Method & verification

The calculation is direct arithmetic. The uncertainty comes from the MPG and fuel-price assumptions you enter, so use a range when future driving conditions are uncertain.
