Fuel-cost formula

gallons = miles ÷ MPG
cost = gallons × price per gallon

Annual cost uses the same math with your yearly mileage. The estimate is most useful when comparing vehicles, commutes or changes in fuel economy.

Why real cost changes

Traffic, idle time, towing, weather, tire pressure, speed and driving style can move real MPG away from the value you enter. Fuel prices also change over time.

Worked example: a 300-mile trip

At 25 mpg, a 300-mile trip uses about 12 gallons. At $3.50 per gallon, the fuel estimate is $42. If the same route returns 20 mpg because of towing or heavy traffic, it uses 15 gallons and costs $52.50. That $10.50 difference is more useful for planning than treating the displayed total as a guaranteed price.

Choose realistic inputs

Use highway MPG for mostly steady highway driving and a lower mixed or city figure for stop-and-go travel. If your vehicle provides trip-computer MPG, compare it with gallons purchased over several fill-ups before relying on it. For a round trip, include the return distance. For annual costs, use your actual yearly mileage rather than multiplying one unusually long trip. This estimate covers fuel only; tolls, parking, maintenance and vehicle depreciation are separate costs.

Why might my fuel bill differ?

The estimate assumes the MPG and fuel price you enter stay constant. Traffic, temperature, driving style and price changes can change the actual cost.
