Compression-ratio formula

CR = (swept volume + clearance volume) ÷ clearance volume

Clearance volume includes the combustion chamber, piston dish or dome, head-gasket volume and deck-clearance volume. In this calculator, a piston dish is entered as a positive volume and a dome as a negative volume.

Static versus dynamic compression

Static compression ratio is purely geometric. Dynamic compression also considers when the intake valve closes, so camshaft timing can dramatically change the effective compression seen by the trapped charge.

Small measurement errors can noticeably change calculated compression ratio. Use measured chamber, piston, gasket and deck values when accuracy matters.

Worked example: clearance volume matters

If one cylinder sweeps 500 cc and the total clearance volume at top dead center is 50 cc, static compression ratio is (500 + 50) ÷ 50 = 11:1. Raise clearance volume to 55 cc and the ratio falls to about 10.09:1. Five cubic centimeters can therefore make a meaningful difference. The swept volume alone cannot establish compression ratio.

Build a complete volume budget

Measure the combustion chamber and piston dish or dome, then account for gasket bore and compressed thickness and the piston position relative to the deck. Keep every volume in the same units. A dome reduces clearance volume; a dish increases it. Real gasket thickness after compression can differ from nominal packaging dimensions. Static ratio is a geometric number and does not by itself establish fuel octane needs or detonation margin, which also depend on cam timing, temperature, boost, combustion design and tuning.

Is static compression ratio the same as cylinder pressure?

No. Static ratio follows the entered volumes. Cam timing, valve closure, boost and operating conditions also affect measured cylinder pressure.
